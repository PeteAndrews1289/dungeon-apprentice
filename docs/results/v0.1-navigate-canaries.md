# Protocol v0.1 Navigate canary results

## Result in one sentence

Three independently initialized agents learned Navigate on unseen dungeons, but all three finished
at the same 75% Navigate retention after promotion and none converted rare stochastic door
interactions into deterministic Unlock competence.

These runs are the first capability evidence in Dungeon Apprentice. They do not establish Unlock
or complete-curriculum mastery, and no untouched final-suite seed was used.

## Frozen run

The three runs used the preregistered seeds `20260722`, `20260723`, and `20260724`. Each began from
random parameters and received 262,144 training actions under protocol `dungeon-apprentice-v0.1`.
Frozen deterministic exams ran every 32,768 trained actions on 40 validation dungeons per unlocked
tier. No configuration changed between seeds.

| Seed | Navigate promotion | Promotion score | Final Navigate | Final Unlock | Final key pickup | Final door open |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 20260722 | 163,840 | 92.5% | 75.0% | 0% | 10.0% | 0% |
| 20260723 | 163,840 | 90.0% | 75.0% | 0% | 0% | 0% |
| 20260724 | 196,608 | 90.0% | 75.0% | 0% | 0% | 0% |

| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |

All three curves rose substantially rather than depending on one lucky run. Mean promotion occurred
at 174,763 trained actions. From each run's promotion exam to its endpoint, the mean retention loss
was 15.8 percentage points; every final Navigate score was exactly 30/40.

## The interaction funnel

The final deterministic Unlock exams recorded 0/120 quest completions and 0/120 opened doors. One
agent collected a key on 4/40 levels; the other two collected none.

The stochastic training record localizes the sequence:

| Seed | Unlock attempts | Key acquired | Door opened | Quest completed |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 20260722 | 369 | 208 | 8 | 8 |
| 20260723 | 364 | 177 | 4 | 4 |
| 20260724 | 245 | 147 | 11 | 10 |
| **Combined** | **978** | **532** | **23** | **22** |

Across all three policies:

- a key was acquired in 54.4% of Unlock attempts;
- a door was opened in 2.35% of Unlock attempts;
- only 4.32% of key-acquisition attempts proceeded to an opened door;
- 95.65% of opened-door attempts then completed the quest.

Key pickup alone is not evidence of a learned key policy; random exploration can encounter and pick
up keys often in this generator. The useful finding is the funnel's shape: the reusable behavior did
not bridge the key-to-door transition, while an opened door was almost always followed by success.

### Uniform-random diagnostic

A reproducible post-run diagnostic made that caution measurable. A uniform random policy played 200
fresh full-Unlock dungeons using environment seeds `30_000_000` through `30_000_199` and one NumPy
action stream seeded `123456`. Curiosity was disabled. It picked up a key in 122/200 episodes (61%),
opened a door in 7/200 (3.5%), completed 0/200, and timed out at 256 actions every time.

The diagnostic sampled uniform integer actions 0–6 for 51,200 total actions. Counts by action were
`[7266, 7389, 7166, 7312, 7248, 7438, 7381]`. It used the same 9 × 9 full-Unlock environment and no
policy update, making it a behavioral baseline rather than another learning run.

The canary policies' 54.4% stochastic key-pickup rate was therefore below this diagnostic random
baseline, while their 2.35% door-opening rate was of the same small order. This reinforces the honest
conclusion: v0.1 learned Navigate, not a reusable Unlock fragment. The diagnostic seed range is now
recorded as consumed and is excluded from all future confirmation and final suites.

## The hidden rehearsal imbalance

Protocol v0.1 sampled 70% newest-tier and 30% earlier-tier **episodes** after promotion. Episodes had
very different lengths. Successful Navigate attempts usually ended after roughly 29–32 actions,
whereas failed Unlock attempts commonly consumed the 256-action horizon. Consequently, Navigate
represented only 4.4%, 4.8%, and 5.7% of recorded post-promotion training transitions across the
three seeds—not the intended 30%.

The sampler therefore exposed the policy to an abrupt distribution change of roughly 95% Unlock
experience. The fixed episode ratio did not meaningfully protect Navigate, and every final Navigate
score fell to 75%. A successor must allocate and verify practice by transitions rather than assuming
that equal episode counts imply equal experience.

This result separates two problems:

1. make complete key-door-exit successes common enough to learn without giving action labels or a
   walkthrough;
2. guarantee a real earlier-skill transition budget and react when retention still weakens.

## Claim

Protocol v0.1 establishes repeatable Navigate learnability under the declared information boundary.
It also supplies replicated negative evidence for episode-count rehearsal and for attempting the
full Unlock distribution immediately after Navigate. It does not show deterministic Unlock
competence, composition, or final-suite generalization.
