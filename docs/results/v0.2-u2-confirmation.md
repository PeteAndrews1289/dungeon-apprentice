# Protocol v0.2 U2 collision-aware confirmation result

> Status: completed frozen result. The preregistered evaluator ran once against the three already
> selected U2 first-mastery checkpoints. Two checkpoints passed every frozen gate. The third
> retained Navigate, Visible Unlock, and Local Unlock, but completed **169/200** Separated Unlock
> cases against a frozen requirement of **170/200**. The strict cohort verdict is therefore
> **`capability_failed`**, not confirmed, and U3 remains blocked.

## Verdict

**U2 is not confirmed across all three lineages.**

The result is unusually close, but the rule was deliberately strict and frozen before any
candidate layout or policy score was opened:

- child `20260737` passed all four lessons overall and in both panels;
- child `20260741` passed all four lessons overall and in both panels;
- child `20260745` passed all three earlier lessons and both U2 panels, but its U2 total was one
  completion below the 170/200 overall gate;
- all **24/24 panel gates** passed, but only **11/12 lesson-level overall gates** passed; and
- the cohort required all three checkpoints to pass every gate. Scores could not be pooled and one
  lineage could not rescue another.

![Two U2 policies pass the entire confirmation while a one-case miss blocks the strict cohort](../assets/v0.2-u2-confirmation-scores.svg)

The immutable machine verdict is `capability_failed`. That is a capability result, not an
engineering crash and not permission to round 169 up to 170. It leaves the stronger development
claim intact—three children learned the U2 development suite—but it does not confirm that result
across the larger collision-aware exam and does not activate U3 Full Unlock.

## The frozen question

The confirmation asked whether the three selected first-mastery archives could retain four
cumulative skills on larger prospectively selected exams without another policy update:

1. Navigate;
2. Visible Unlock U0;
3. Local Unlock U1; and
4. Separated Unlock U2.

Each lesson contained 200 accepted cases split in acceptance order into fixed 100-case panels A and
B. Navigate required 170/200 overall and 85/100 in each panel. U0, U1, and U2 required 170/200
overall and 80/100 in each panel. The strict verdict required every checkpoint to pass every
overall and panel gate.

The evaluator used deterministic actions, reset recurrent state for every case, disabled learning,
and made no online model calls. Candidate acceptance was policy-blind: all four exams qualified and
were frozen before any checkpoint was scored.

### What the missed cases suggest

Milestones are diagnostics only—the full quest completion remains the success criterion—but they
help localize the remaining weakness:

| Child | U2 key pickups | U2 doors opened | U2 completions | Door given key | Completion given door |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `20260737` | 188 | 185 | 184 | 98.40% | 99.46% |
| `20260741` | 195 | 189 | 188 | 96.92% | 99.47% |
| `20260745` | 188 | 172 | 169 | 91.49% | 98.26% |

For child `20260745`, 188 of 200 cases reached the key, 172 opened the door, and 169 completed the
quest. Once it opened the door it almost always finished. The larger gap lies between acquiring the
key and locating or operating the door. That is a useful diagnostic target for a future,
prospectively frozen U2 remediation design; it is not evidence that a particular algorithmic fix
will work, and it cannot justify retesting this already opened exam.

Its behavior was also less efficient than the two passing peers. Of 1,387 pickup, drop, and toggle
actions, 1,027 (74.0%) were ineffective; 968 actions were toggles. It averaged 5.135 ineffective
interactions per U2 case, versus 0.71 and 0.19, and used 2.526 policy actions per oracle action,
versus 2.105 and 1.905. The same interaction-heavy pattern appeared on U0 and U1 while Navigate
remained comparatively clean, localizing the weakness to learned interaction contexts rather than
ordinary movement. Those diagnostics are consistent with a less stable key-to-door routine, but
they do not establish why a recurrent policy made any individual mistake.

The development history shows that this brittleness was already measurable before confirmation.
Between child `20260745`'s first passing U2 exam and the second consecutive pass that selected its
first-mastery archive, another 32,768 training actions reduced U2 from 76/80 to 72/80 while average
ineffective interactions rose from 2.49 to 6.54 and toggle actions rose from 238 to 554. On those
same two boundaries, U0 moved from 80/80 to 76/80 with toggles rising from 80 to 600, and U1 moved
from 80/80 to 77/80 with toggles rising from 83 to 456. Every score still cleared its frozen gate,
so the checkpoint was selected correctly under the preregistered rule. In hindsight, however, the
second pass was measurably less stable than the first. This supports investigating stability-aware
checkpoint rules in a future protocol; it does not permit retroactively substituting the earlier
checkpoint.

The same child succeeded across every initial-visibility stratum, but less robustly than the other
two policies:

| Initial U2 visibility | Cases | Child `20260745` successes |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Key hidden, door hidden | 74 | 62 (83.78%) |
| Key hidden, door visible | 36 | 33 (91.67%) |
| Key visible, door hidden | 57 | 48 (84.21%) |
| Key visible, door visible | 33 | 26 (78.79%) |

These are post-result diagnostics on one shared 200-case exam, not separately powered gates. The
lowest observed stratum was unexpectedly the both-visible group, so the result does not support a
simple claim that initial invisibility alone caused the miss.

## Two pre-claim launch defects

The canonical confirmation attempt was preceded by fail-closed launch work. None of these stops
opened a reserved candidate or scored a policy.

First, the newly added console shortcut was not yet installed in the existing virtual environment,
so the initial shell command never entered Python. Direct module invocation then exposed the first
actual launch defect: the historical qualification-anchor adapter correctly authenticated the
frozen U2 training tag but returned the newer evaluator commit in its public record. The
qualification binding rejected that mismatch before a confirmation tag, canonical claim, or
15.2-million candidate existed. The adapter was corrected to report the anchored training commit
when verifying historical evidence, and a regression test now covers verification from newer clean
source.

Second, the installed Apple Git returns status 128 for an absent `show-ref --verify` lookup unless
the quiet form is used. The preregistration publisher treated that platform-specific result as
indeterminate and again stopped before tag creation, claim publication, or candidate access. Both
tag publishers were changed to the portable quiet form. The full 299-test suite and 300-level
mechanical qualification passed again before the canonical attempt was allowed to claim the
streams.

These are part of the result's narrative because fail-closed behavior worked: an operational
shortcut miss and two launch defects delayed the exam, but they did not contaminate it.

## Interpretation

The experiment produced mixed rather than empty evidence.

Two independently inherited policies generalized strongly: their U2 confirmation scores were
92% and 94%, and they retained every earlier lesson. The third also retained all earlier skills and
completed 84.5% of U2 cases. This is consistent with a real learned capability that is not yet
reliable across every lineage under the project's declared standard.

The one-case miss is not evidence of a dramatic collapse. Under a simple Binomial model with
`n = 200` and true success probability `p = 0.85`, the probability of observing 169 or fewer
successes is 45.1489%. That calculation is descriptive rather than a replacement decision test:
the protocol deliberately chose a discrete 170-success gate, so 169 remains a failure even though
it is statistically ordinary near an 85% underlying rate.

The one-case margin matters in two different ways:

1. **Scientifically, it says the failed lineage is close.** A future design can focus on robust
   key-to-door search rather than rebuilding Navigate or the complete interaction sequence.
2. **Procedurally, it changes nothing.** The 170 threshold and all-three rule were frozen in
   advance. Calling 169 a pass after seeing it would erase the value of preregistration.

The development result and confirmation result therefore coexist:

| Evidence stage | Result | Narrow conclusion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Three 80-case development lineages | 3/3 mastered | U2 can be learned repeatedly under curriculum-guiding development exams |
| One 200-case collision-aware confirmation per frozen checkpoint | 2/3 passed; third missed U2 by 1 | U2 generalization is promising but not confirmed across all three lineages |
| U3 Full Unlock | Not opened | Remains blocked |

## Limits

- The U2 children were warm-started from separately trained and confirmed U1 parents. This tests
  cumulative transfer, not learning U2 from random parameters.
- All three policies faced the same four accepted exams. Their training lineages are independent,
  but the 200 cases per lesson are one shared evaluation sample and must not be pooled.
- The selector conditions the exam on solvability, exact mechanics, uniqueness, and declared
  reference exclusions. The result applies to that bounded 9 × 9 Separated Unlock distribution,
  not arbitrary dungeons.
- U0 is a finite retention generator. Its accepted cases were exact-validation-disjoint, but
  training-history overlap was diagnostic rather than a gating exclusion.
- Completed logged training episodes were excluded. Twelve layouts active at the final U2
  optimizer boundaries were not persisted as episode records, so collision with those 12 layouts
  cannot be ruled out. This limitation was frozen before confirmation and remains explicit.
- The 200-case overall proportions have sampling uncertainty. That uncertainty is useful for
  interpretation but does not replace the preregistered discrete gate.
- The post-result visibility and milestone breakdowns are diagnostic. They were not separately
  powered hypotheses and cannot authorize a tuned retry on the same opened candidates.
- Nothing here establishes U3, Retrieve, unrestricted planning, combat, inventory strategy,
  party behavior, the untouched final suite, or a complete game.
